# Where Experts Disagree
## AI-Powered SEO Content Production

---

# Overview

SEO is an evolving discipline, and experienced professionals often disagree on implementation details. Rather than treating these differences as contradictions, this playbook evaluates each perspective and explains the reasoning behind the final recommendation.

---

# Disagreement 1 — Should AI Generate Most of the Content?

## Expert A — Mike King

Mike King views AI as a powerful productivity tool that can significantly improve research, planning, and drafting when used strategically.

**Sources**

- Mike King – *Relevance, Google & the End of SEO As We Know It*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eS4kfPUfFog

---

## Expert B — Lily Ray

Lily Ray warns against relying heavily on AI-generated content. She emphasizes that content should demonstrate genuine expertise, experience, and trustworthiness.

**Sources**

- Lily Ray – *Google Core Updates, GEO, E-E-A-T, and the Future of Search*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2htSIT0HLjs

---

## My Decision

I support a balanced approach.

AI should accelerate repetitive work such as brainstorming, outlining, and drafting. However, every article should undergo human editing, fact-checking, and quality review before publication. This approach combines efficiency with credibility and aligns with Google's emphasis on helpful, trustworthy content.

# Disagreement 2 — Are Backlinks Still the Primary Ranking Factor?

## Expert A — Matt Diggity

Matt Diggity places significant emphasis on backlinks as a competitive advantage for improving rankings.

**Sources**

- Matt Diggity – *I Did AI SEO for ChatGPT and Google AI. Here's What Happened...*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GBlHObjOrY

---

## Expert B — Kevin Indig

Kevin Indig argues that AI-powered search is shifting the focus toward content quality, user satisfaction, and brand authority rather than backlinks alone.

**Sources**

- Kevin Indig – *Professional Spotlight with Kevin Indig: SEO, AI, and Product-Led Growth*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFqNOCWbF-4

---

## My Decision

I believe backlinks remain valuable, but they should support—not replace—high-quality content. Sustainable SEO depends on producing authoritative resources that naturally earn links rather than relying on aggressive link-building campaigns.

# Disagreement 3 — What Should Define SEO Success?

## Expert A — Brian Dean

Brian Dean emphasizes improving rankings and increasing organic traffic through proven SEO strategies.

**Sources**

- Brian Dean – *How Backlinko's Brian Dean Used SEO Optimization & Skyscraper Technique to Grow His Website Traffic*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D0Oc25pxzGA

---

## Expert B — Rand Fishkin

Rand Fishkin encourages measuring broader business outcomes such as brand visibility, engagement, and user behaviour instead of focusing only on rankings.

**Sources**

- Rand Fishkin – *Near Memo: AI Visibility vs Google Ranking — 2026 Predictions*
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_5oc-O1B8k

---

## My Decision

I agree more closely with Rand Fishkin's perspective. Rankings are useful indicators, but they should not be the ultimate objective. Successful SEO should be measured through meaningful outcomes such as qualified traffic, engagement, conversions, and long-term brand visibility.

---

# Overall Conclusion

The research demonstrates that experienced SEO professionals often agree on core principles while differing in implementation strategies. After evaluating these viewpoints, this playbook adopts a balanced approach:

- Use AI to improve productivity, but keep humans responsible for quality.
- Earn backlinks through valuable content rather than depending on link-building alone.
- Measure SEO success using business outcomes, not rankings alone.

These decisions reflect the evidence collected throughout the research repository and prioritize sustainable, user-focused SEO practices.
